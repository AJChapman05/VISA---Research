# Lab 10 - Pro-Forma: Visa Inc. (NYSE: V)

**Question:** What are five years of Visa's statements worth, built from assumptions I can defend?

All dollar amounts below are USD millions. Visa's fiscal year ends September 30.

## Company-specific line

Visa is an asset-light payment network, not an inventory business. Its company-specific line is **client incentives**: they reduce gross revenue to reported net revenue and were $15,751 in FY2025. The model forecasts net revenue rather than inventing inventory, gross profit, floor-plan financing, or an ABG-style SG&A line. It holds the net working-capital effect of client-incentive, settlement, and accrued-liability balances flat; that is visible in the cash-flow statement as equal changes in other operating assets and liabilities.

This is a valuation question, not a recommendation.

## Fresh-eyes review

**Partner attack on my Visa model:** Why use a 10% net-revenue growth judgment when FY2025 client incentives rose faster than net revenue and can reduce reported revenue conversion?

**My answer:** The 10% assumption is below the 10.7% FY2023–FY2025 reported CAGR and below the 14% Q3 FY2026 growth rate, so it is not an acceleration forecast. I would lower it if client incentives continued to outgrow gross revenue or if payment-volume and cross-border growth weakened; I would raise it only with sustained evidence that value-added services and cross-border growth offset that pressure.

**My attack on my partner’s Meta model:** Meta’s revenue depends heavily on advertising demand and engagement. Why did you choose your revenue-growth assumption, and what evidence would make you lower it?

**My partner’s answer:** I based my growth assumption on Meta’s recent revenue trend and its continued growth in ad impressions, pricing, and AI-driven advertising tools. I would lower it if advertising demand weakened, engagement growth slowed, regulation limited ad targeting, or competition reduced Meta’s ability to maintain ad pricing.

## Reflection

The label I would defend longest is the historical 31.9% D&A-to-opening-PP&E ratio because both numerator and denominator come directly from the same filing and have a clear accounting relationship. The surprising filing number was $15.751 billion of client incentives in FY2025: it is large enough that small changes in incentive economics can matter even for a company with Visa's high margins.
